The Motor City is building a bike network. Here's how far it reaches, where the Joe Louis Greenway stands, and where riders still get hurt.

1148 miles of bike routes

Detroit's dedicated bike facilities add up to about 148 miles. Painted lanes are the most common type, followed by protected lanes and off-street paths.

    Turn each type on and off. Hover a route for its name.

    2Protected or painted?

    A painted line is easy to drive over. Posts, curbs or planters are not. 41 miles of Detroit's lanes have physical protection, mostly flexible posts. They're concentrated downtown, in Midtown and along major corridors.

    3The Joe Louis Greenway

    4Within a quarter mile

    A quarter mile is about a five-minute walk with a bike, or under two minutes riding. About 34% of Detroit lies within a quarter mile of a dedicated bike facility.

    66 of 205 neighborhoods have none within a quarter mile anywhere inside them.

    Darker neighborhoods are better connected. Click one to see its share.

    5Where riders get hurt

    640crashes involving a bicyclist, 2020–2024
    19deaths
    83serious injuries

    75% of those crashes inside the city happened more than 100 feet from any dedicated bike facility, on streets where riders share space with cars.

    Larger dots mark crashes with a death or serious injury.

    6Slowing the streets

    Bike lanes aren't the only fix. The city's Slow Streets program has added speed humps on 4,215 residential street segments, shown in thin teal. 23 Safe Streets corridors, shown in black, are being designed for added protection.

    Together with 81 MoGo bike-share stations and 49 public repair stands, they're the building blocks of a city where more people can choose to ride.

    Data & method

    From the City of Detroit Open Data Portal: Bike Lanes, Joe Louis Greenway Route Segments, Traffic Crashes (bicycle-involved, 2020–2024), Detroit Slow Streets, Safe Streets for Detroit Corridors, MoGo Stations, Bicycle Repair Stations, and Current City of Detroit Neighborhoods.

    "Dedicated" means painted, buffered or protected lanes, shared-use paths, and open Greenway segments. Shared-lane markings and signed routes are shown but not counted. Miles are summed from each segment as recorded, so overlapping segments may be counted twice. Reach is measured on a 150-meter grid of sample points. The city's Greenways layer is not used, because it doesn't distinguish built routes from planned ones.
